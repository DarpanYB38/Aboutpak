🗳️ Political System Overview

Pakistan operates as a federal parliamentary republic, comprising three main branches: the executive, legislative, and judiciary. The President serves as the ceremonial head of state, while the Prime Minister holds executive powers as the head of government. The bicameral Parliament consists of the National Assembly (lower house) and the Senate (upper house). The judiciary is independent, with the Supreme Court at its apex.

🏛️ Major Political Parties

Pakistan Muslim League (N) 🏙️

Founded in 1993 by Nawaz Sharif, the PML-N is a center-right party advocating for economic liberalization and infrastructural development. It has a strong base in Punjab and has produced multiple Prime Ministers, including Nawaz Sharif and Shehbaz Sharif.

Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) 🏏

Established in 1996 by former cricketer Imran Khan, PTI positions itself as a centrist party focusing on anti-corruption and social justice. It gained significant popularity leading to a victory in the 2018 general elections, with Imran Khan serving as Prime Minister until 2022.

Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) 🌹

Founded in 1967 by Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, the PPP is a center-left party with a stronghold in Sindh. It emphasizes social justice and democratic socialism. Notable leaders include Benazir Bhutto and Asif Ali Zardari.

⚖️ Other Political Entities

Jamiat Ulema-e-Islam (F) 🕌

An Islamic conservative party led by Maulana Fazlur Rehman, focusing on implementing Sharia law and representing religious seminaries.

Muttahida Qaumi Movement (MQM) 🏢

Primarily representing the Urdu-speaking Muhajir community in urban Sindh, especially Karachi, MQM advocates for the rights of urban middle-class populations.

Awami National Party (ANP) 🚩

A secular, leftist party with a strong base among Pashtuns in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, promoting Pashtun nationalism and provincial autonomy.
